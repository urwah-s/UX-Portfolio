# Photo Sharing App – Notification Design Study

## Overview
This project was completed in a team of four as part of an advanced Human-Computer Interaction (HCI) course. The goal was to design and evaluate a mobile photo-sharing experience that better supports group coordination after events.

We focused on a common real-world problem: friends often fail to share photos after events due to forgetfulness, missed messages, or lack of coordination, resulting in lost or scattered media.

---

## Problem
Our initial field study revealed key challenges in group photo sharing:
- Photos are scattered across chats, links, and personal devices
- Users forget or delay responding to photo requests
- Requests are often missed or ignored
- Social friction (e.g., being busy, not noticing messages) disrupts coordination

While existing tools are functional, they do not effectively support timely participation and group collaboration.

---

## Solution
We designed a medium-fidelity prototype that centralizes event-based photo sharing and explores how interface design can improve user engagement.

### Key Focus: Notification Design
This project specifically investigates:

> How can notification design encourage users to respond to photo-sharing requests without overwhelming them?

## Research & Experiment

### Methodology
- 8 participants (ages 19–30)
- Remote usability study using think-aloud protocol + interviews
- Between-subjects experimental design

### Variables
- **Independent Variable:** Notification frequency & visibility
    - High-frequency (pop-ups) vs. low-frequency (subtle badge)
- **Dependent Variable:** Response time to photo request

---

## Key Findings

### Quantitative Results
- High-frequency notifications → faster responses (33s avg)
- Low-frequency notifications → slower responses (62.7s avg)
- Statistically significant difference (**p < 0.05**)
- Large effect size (**Cohen’s d = 2.18**)

More visible notifications significantly improved responsiveness

---

### Qualitative Insights
- **Low-frequency notifications:**
    - Often missed or overlooked
    - Preferred for being non-intrusive

- **High-frequency notifications:**
    - Highly noticeable but sometimes overwhelming
    - Could disrupt task flow and feel intrusive

---

## Design Insights
- Visibility improves responsiveness, but too much intrusiveness harms user experience
- Users benefit from clear, persistent cues rather than disruptive interruptions
- Effective design must balance:
    - Attention (visibility)
    - User autonomy (non-intrusiveness)

---

## Design Recommendations
- Use persistent but lightweight notification cues instead of repeated pop-ups
- Keep request context visible during task flow to reduce memory load
- Standardize notification design to avoid confusion with system alerts
- Improve interface clarity (e.g., consistent content ordering)
